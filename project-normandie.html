Private Commission

Domaine Rural

Location Normandie
Scope Estate restoration & redesign
Type Historic property, 12 ha
Status Completed, ongoing stewardship
Design intent

Restoring a historic
landscape without
erasing its age.

This commission involved the full landscape assessment and phased redesign of a 19th-century Normandie estate. The property had fallen into gradual neglect and the new owners — a family intending to use it as their primary residence — wanted it brought back to life without erasing its character or history.

The first step was a full survey and reading of what remained: old parkland trees of extraordinary scale, a kitchen garden with original walls still largely intact, an orchard, several outbuildings, and traces of former formal planting near the house. This existing structure became the basis for all subsequent decisions.

The parkland was cleared, thinned, and replanted with a long-term planting strategy — introducing new trees in dialogue with the old, and restoring meadow grass under the canopy. The kitchen garden was redesigned for contemporary productive use, retaining its walled enclosure but rethinking the internal arrangement around the family's actual needs.

Closer to the house, a simple formal garden was introduced — restrained geometry, clipped hornbeam, and a grass terrace that connects the building to the landscape beyond. The intent was coherence, not restoration of something that no longer exists.
