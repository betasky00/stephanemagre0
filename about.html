The practice

About

Stéphane Magre designs private landscapes for residences where discretion, balance, and atmosphere matter as much as the garden itself.

Philosophy

I believe a garden is not separate from its house. It is a continuation of it — the same intentions, the same quality of thought, simply expressed in a different material.

My work begins with careful listening. Before any design, I spend time understanding the site: its orientation, its history, its relationship to the surrounding landscape, and the way the clients live. A garden designed without this understanding is just a plan — it has no roots.

I am interested in proportion and stillness. In the way light moves across a terrace at different hours. In the difference between a hedge that encloses and one that frames. In the long arc of how a garden changes over ten years, and how those changes should be anticipated from the beginning.

Principles

How I think
about a site.

Site before style

Every site has a logic — a climate, a soil, a relationship to light and water, an existing ecology. Good design begins by reading this logic and working with it, not against it. Style follows from that understanding, not the other way around.

Proportion and restraint

In most gardens, the instinct is to add. My instinct is to remove — to find the minimum number of elements that produce the maximum quality of space. Restraint is not absence. It is precision.

The long view

A garden at planting is a drawing. A garden at five years is beginning to become itself. A garden at fifteen years is what it was always meant to be. I design for that fifteen-year version, not the day of completion.

Materials as language

Stone, water, gravel, clipped hedges, loose planting — each material carries meaning. I choose them for their specific qualities in a specific place, not because they are fashionable or easy. The combination must feel inevitable.

Architecture as partner

The best gardens I have worked on began with a conversation with the architect. When landscape and architecture speak the same language — the same proportions, the same palette, the same sense of space — the result feels whole rather than assembled.

Discretion in practice

I work with private clients on private properties. Discretion is not just a professional courtesy — it is a condition of the work. The gardens I design are not public statements. They are private worlds, and they should remain so.

My role

Design, coordination,
and coherence.

I am the designer and the project lead. I produce the concept, develop the design through all its stages, and oversee the execution to ensure the result matches the intention. This means I am present on site, I manage the relationship with contractors, and I make the decisions that preserve the quality of the work.

I do not operate a large team or handle a high volume of commissions. This is deliberate. Every project I accept receives my full attention.

Collaboration

A small network
of trusted partners.

Over many years of practice, I have built relationships with a small group of specialist contractors — stonemasons, earthwork teams, planting specialists, irrigation engineers — whose quality and discretion I trust entirely.

I work regularly with architects and interior designers who require a landscape partner capable of operating at the same level as their own practice. If you are an architect with a relevant project, I welcome the conversation.

Background

Trained in landscape,
shaped by practice.

I trained in landscape architecture in France and completed my education with time spent in the Netherlands and the United Kingdom — two traditions of garden and landscape design that have shaped my thinking considerably.

After early years working within established practices on larger-scale projects, I moved to private commissions. I have worked in this way for fifteen years, building a practice that is deliberately small, deliberately selective, and entirely focused on private residential work.

I am based in Paris but work across France and, selectively, internationally. I am fluent in French and English.
